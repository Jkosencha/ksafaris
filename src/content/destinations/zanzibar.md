Zanzibar is one of the most popular destinations in Tanzania — there aren’t many places in the world
like these islands off the coast, with incredible diving and Africa’s answer to the Caribbean. The
Zanzibar Archipelago lies in the Indian Ocean, about 25–50 km off the mainland, and is made up of
several small islands and two large ones: Unguja (commonly called Zanzibar) and Pemba. The capital,
Zanzibar City, is on Unguja, and its historic centre, Stone Town, is a World Heritage Site.

## Beaches

Zanzibar is bursting with beaches — the ideal way to relax after a safari. The east coast has a
continuous coral reef with white sandy beaches lined with coconut palms and small coves sheltered from
the wind. Non-tidal beaches along the north-west coast, between Nungwi and Kendwa, allow all-day
swimming, while the coral cliffs and lagoon coves of the south-west offer an escape to quieter spots,
with secret beaches perfect for a picnic.

## Stone Town

Stone Town is a Swahili coastal trading town with an intriguing history: explorers, exotic spices, sea
traders and sultans all left their mark, and many historic sites remain intact. Its coral-stone
buildings and narrow streets carry the imprint of many cultures and traditions.
