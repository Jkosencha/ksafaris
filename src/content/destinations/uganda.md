Uganda, commonly known as the “Pearl of Africa”, has a fairly constant climate of warm days and cool
nights. With Mount Elgon in the east and the Rwenzori Mountains in the west, the rest of the country is
tropical, with daytime temperatures averaging around 26°C. The highlands are considerably cooler,
especially at night.

Uganda is home to two of Africa’s most important freshwater bodies — Lake Victoria and the River Nile —
along with the dense Mabira Forest, Bwindi Impenetrable Forest, Kibale Forest, and the mountains of
Elgon and Rwenzori with their caves, gorges, hot springs and waterfalls.

## Gorilla trekking

Most visitors come to Uganda to track the endangered mountain gorillas in the south-west of the country,
in Bwindi Impenetrable Forest or Mgahinga Gorilla National Park. Despite their fierce reputation,
gorillas are peaceable primates, and the chance to view them up close is thrilling and awe-inspiring.

Tracking generally involves a fairly strenuous walk through forest or dense undergrowth on steep slopes,
led by guides who take trekkers to where the gorillas were found the day before. Tracking can take
anywhere from three to eight hours, and groups may stay with the gorillas for one hour once they are
found, to limit disturbance and the possible transmission of disease. Because gorillas are so close to
humans genetically, anyone with even a common cold is not allowed to join a group.

## History and culture

Visit the Uganda Martyrs Shrine at Namugongo, the Kasubi Tombs, the botanical gardens on the shores of
Lake Victoria, and learn about the culture and traditions of the Buganda kingdom.
