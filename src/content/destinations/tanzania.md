The largest country in East Africa, Tanzania boasts the highest mountain on the continent, the exotic
spice islands of historic Zanzibar, and the famous Serengeti National Park, whose seemingly endless
plains stage one of the greatest spectacles of animal behaviour: the annual migration of millions of
wildebeest and zebra, followed by their predators.

The Great Rift Valley gives rise to the unique geological formations of the magnificent Ngorongoro
Crater and Mount Kilimanjaro. Tanzania is richly endowed with animal and bird species and offers some
of the finest game viewing on the continent. A dusty safari into the vast wilderness is superbly
complemented by time on the Zanzibar islands, with white palm-fringed beaches, coral gardens and
historic Stone Town.

Tanzania is home to many different ethnic groups and cultures, from the red-clad Maasai herders of the
Serengeti plains to the people of Zanzibar’s Stone Town. The warmth and smiling faces of its friendly
people will touch the heart of every traveller.

## Good to know

- **Health:** take medical advice at least three weeks before travelling. A yellow fever certificate is
  required if you arrive from a country with a risk of yellow fever, and malaria precautions are
  recommended all year. Comprehensive medical insurance is advised.
- **Tipping:** around 10% in better restaurants. Guides, porters and cooks on safari appreciate tips at
  your discretion.
- **Airports:** Kilimanjaro International (JRO), 40 km east of Arusha, is the usual gateway for northern
  safaris; Julius Nyerere International (DAR) serves Dar es Salaam.
- **Time:** GMT+3.
